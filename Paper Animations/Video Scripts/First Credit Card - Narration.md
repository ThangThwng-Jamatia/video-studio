# How To Use Your First Credit Card — Narration
# Video Format: Shorts (9:16), Day Series 3D style
# LENGTH: 50 s (~122 words)

Congrats. You're eighteen, and the bank just handed you a credit card with a thousand-dollar limit. Your friend says, "Bro, that's free money." Your friend is wrong. Survive the next thirty days, and this card builds your future. Mess up, and it gets more expensive than a girlfriend.

Day one. Pizza time. Rule one: only swipe money that's already in your bank. The card is a remote, not a wallet.

Day ten. Keep the balance under three hundred. Maxing it out scares lenders, like your mom seeing your screen time.

Day thirty. The bill lands. Pay the full amount, not the minimum. Full means zero interest. Minimum means over twenty percent, eating you alive.

Set autopay. Never miss a due date.

Do this for one year, and banks start treating you like a millionaire.

## Fact sources
- Paying the statement balance in full by the due date avoids interest on purchases (grace period): CFPB, "What is a grace period for a credit card?"
- Keeping utilization under ~30% is standard guidance; amounts owed ≈ 30% of a FICO score: myFICO, "What's in my FICO Scores".
- Payment history ≈ 35% of a FICO score: myFICO (same page).
- Average credit card APR above 20% (Fed G.19, 2024–2026, ~21–23% on accounts assessed interest).
