## Inputs (any one of these)
1. **A script**: an `.md` file in `Paper Animations/Video Scripts/`. If it contains narration, use it as written (fix only obvious typos). If it is a **structure brief** (format, length, chapters with timings, points and takeaways, like `Basic Economics.md`), write the narration to that structure and timing yourself, save it as `<name> - Narration.md` next to it, and go straight on to voice + build (the user asked for the whole video, so no "go" needed). `# Video Format` / `# LENGTH` lines set format and length.
2. **A topic**: write the script yourself.
3. **An inspo video**: analyse it (see below), then write a **new** script on that theme.
**Length:** the user gives a target length. Narration runs about **145 words per minute** (the pilot was 113 words in 47 s). Write to `minutes × 145` words, ±5%. If no length is given, ask.

## Originality (hard rule)
- From inspo videos, take only the topic area, structure (hook → numbered lessons → takeaway), pacing, palette mood and kinds of shots. **Never transcribe, translate or closely paraphrase their narration**, and never redraw their characters, mascots or logos. "Copy the script", "recreate it exactly" and similar requests get an original script on the same theme, and you tell the user why in one line. This protects their channel from copyright strikes.
- Characters come from the kit's `Person` rig. Sam (mustard cap, freckles, orange striped tee) is the user's recurring lead. Add new characters by varying colours, hair, accessories and props, never by copying someone else's design.

## Script voice: entertainment first, lesson inside (user rule 2026-10-24)
Scripts are **entertainment that teaches**, not lectures. The money lesson stays real and accurate, but it arrives through jokes, absurd what-ifs and relatable life moments.
- **Funny, relatable hooks and mid-video bits.** Absurd comparisons, wild hypotheticals and school/teen/dating life. Examples of the vibe (the user's own): "Which is more expensive: a GF or a car?", "Elon Musk just gave his 1 trillion to you", "Are your GF's mood swings more dangerous than stock market swings?", "You became a millionaire. Now how does everyone treat you at school?". Invent fresh ones per topic in this spirit (mom checking your screen time, a friend saying "bro, it's free money", the group-chat reaction, the teacher's face). Use at least **one in the hook plus one every ~15–20 s** in the body; a callback to the hook joke at the end lands well.
- Keep jokes punching at situations, never at real private people or groups; celebrities appear only in obviously fictional hypotheticals, never as a fake quote or endorsement.
- Facts never bend for a joke: the punchline sits next to the true number, not instead of it.
- **Shorts: give the viewer a role + a problem in the first 2 lines.** Second person ("You're 18, the bank just handed you…"), with stakes that can only be resolved by watching to the end ("Survive the next 30 days and…"). The body is the viewer solving it step by step; the answer/payoff lands in the last 5 s. No "today we'll learn about…" openers, ever.
- Longs: same voice; each chapter opens on a mini-scenario or joke before the lesson.

## Analysing an inspo video
Don't watch all of it. Sample it: `ffprobe` for the duration, then a contact sheet of the first 2–3 min (`ffmpeg -t 180 -i f -vf "fps=1/8,scale=480:-1,tile=4x4" -frames:v 1 grid.jpg`), plus one sheet from the middle. Note shot length, scene types, chapter-card style, palette and on-screen text density. If the topic is unclear from the frames, whisper the first 60 s for **your own understanding only** (never reuse the wording).

## Workflow
1. **Script.** Write or read it. Hook (≤2 sentences), then numbered sections, then a one-line takeaway. Short spoken sentences with concrete nouns (things you can draw: a car, a bucket, a $10 bill). Show the user the script and wait for "go", unless they supplied the script themselves.
2. **Voice.** `vidiq_voiceover_list_voices`, then `vidiq_voiceover_generate` (max 5000 characters per call, about 800 words / 5.5 min; split longer scripts at paragraph breaks into parts 1..n), then poll `vidiq_job_poll` until it's completed. The default voice is **George** (`JBFqnCBsd6RMkjVDRZzb`, warm storyteller) unless the user picks another. Write "Number one." rather than "#1." in the text you send for the voice.
   Multi-part voice: download each part and `ffmpeg -f concat` them, with 0.4 s silence between, into one mp3 before prep.
3. **Prep.** From `_engine/`: `scripts/paper_prep.sh <slug> "<mp3 url or path>"`. This writes `public/jobs/<slug>/voice.mp3`, `voice.json`, `words.txt` (every word with its start time) and `music.wav` (generated bed, voice length + 2.5 s). If the user has added a track to `Music/`, use that instead.
4. **Scene plan.** One scene per sentence or idea, **2–5 s each** (the pilot used 13 scenes in 48 s). Each scene has: a background (paper / street / colour card), characters with poses, and 1–4 **beats**, where props pop, crosses strike or text stamps land on the exact word time from `words.txt`. Number cards ("#1") get a solid colour card. Draw nouns literally and turn abstract ideas into icons plus 2–4 words of text.
5. **Build** `_engine/src/paper/<Pascal>.tsx`. Shared helpers in the kit: `Label`, `Stamp`, `Fly`, `ValueCard`, `LineChart`, `NumberCard`, `TakeawayCard`, `Floor`, `Queue`, plus props (pizza, shop, bank, exchange board, ballot, jar, tree, egg basket, factory, …). SFX: `python3 scripts/paper_sfx.py src/paper/<Pascal>.tsx src/paper/<camel>Sfx.ts --ding <money moments>` generates the cue list from the beat times. Copy the structure of `PaperPilot.tsx`: `SCENES: Scene[]` (absolute `from`/`to` seconds, each scene a component that takes `{t}`), `SFX: Sfx[]`, and export `<PaperStage job="jobs/<slug>" sec={...} scenes={SCENES} sfx={SFX} />`. Register it in `src/Root.tsx` (`fps 30`, 1920×1080, `durationInFrames = sec*30`). `sec` = voice length + about 1.5 s.
   New reusable props and backgrounds go into `kit.tsx` (exported), not into the video file.
6. **QA stills** before the full render: `npx remotion still src/index.ts <Id> <scratch>/x.png --frame=N` on 2–3 busy frames. Look for overlaps (text on faces), off-screen props and props that read wrong. `npx tsc --noEmit -p .` must be clean.
7. **Render** (unsandboxed, from `_engine/`, see CLAUDE.md): `npx remotion render src/index.ts <Id> "../Paper Animations/Completed/Long/<Title>.mp4" --concurrency=4` (shorts → `Completed/Shorts`, 1080×1920). For videos **over about 4 min**, render in segments with `scripts/render_long_segments.sh` (see the long-video-editor skill). Then build a contact sheet of about 13 frames across the video, check it, `open` the file and report what's rough.
8. **Thumbnails (mandatory after every completed video).** Long-form: make **3 thumbnail variations**. **Short-form (9:16): make only 1** (user rule 2026-10-23), the strongest layout (usually B, contrast split), as `<Slug>-thumb.png`, 1080×1920. Save long-form ones to `Paper Animations/Completed/Thumbnails/<Slug>-thumb-A/B/C.png` (1280×720; Shorts 1080×1920 cover). Before designing, look at `Inspirations/Thumbnails/` (tile them with ffmpeg) for any new inspos **and at any thumbnail the user made themselves in `Completed/Thumbnails/`** (e.g. `Economics 101_ Finance Foundations.png`): their own thumbs are the strongest style signal. **Current preferred style (2026-10-22): dark-green collage** (`#16301F` bg, chalk-line chart/arrow/pie doodles), huge white title on black blocks, torn-paper sub-labels, a lime `#B8E06A` highlight block, and a big close-up Sam with a white sticker outline + drop shadow (`_engine/src/paper/ThumbsStocks.tsx` has the `sticker`/`drop` filters and `Block`/`Paper` helpers; copy it per video). Never put real banknotes, real people's portraits or brand logos in a thumbnail. Use the three layouts that worked (`_engine/src/paper/Thumbs.tsx`, copy it per video as `Thumbs<Pascal>.tsx` or parametrise):
   - **A, title punch:** brand-colour background with faint kit-icon doodle wallpaper, a 2–4 word huge title (Fredoka 700, paper fill, ink stroke), a small tag pill above, a hook pill below, and Sam with a strong emotion (`o` / `grin`) at the right.
   - **B, contrast split:** two halves (sage vs red, or good vs bad), label pills, one character per side with opposite moods and arrows, and a bridging pill in the middle.
   - **C, editorial chart/visual:** paper background, a two-line headline (ink + terra), a red spiking chart or one big visual metaphor from the video, and Sam pointing.
   Rules: readable at phone size (title ≥140 px on the 1920 canvas), max ~6 words total, never cover a face or a sign with text or characters, and use our cast and palette only (inspos give layout ideas, never copy their characters or art). Render with `npx remotion still … --image-format=png`, tile the three for a QA look, fix overlaps, `open` the folder, and recommend one.
9. **Log** the job in `_engine/edit-log.md`.

## The look (kit defaults, keep consistent)
- Palette `C`: paper `#F4EBD6`, ink outlines `#3B2F2A` (5–6 px), terra `#C8553D`, mustard `#E3A32B`, sage `#7C9A7E`, navy `#2E4057`, cash greens. Colour cards (`terra`, `mustard`, `sage`) for chapter numbers and lessons.
- Fredoka 600/700 for all text. On-screen scene text is short (≤6 words).
- **Captions: always ON (user rule 2026-10-23), for both shorts and longs.** Small, simple, **one line only** at the bottom (dark rounded pill, cream Fredoka 600, ~40 px on 1080p), showing the narration phrase by phrase so viewers can follow by reading alone. Built into `PaperStage` (`PaperCaptions` in the kit) from `public/jobs/<slug>/voice.json`; phrases break at sentence ends, commas and pauses, max 42 chars (16:9) or pass `captionChars={26}` for 9:16. Keep scene text and key props out of the bottom ~130 px so captions never cover them. Only turn off (`captions={false}`) if the user asks.
- Motion: `Pop` (back-ease scale-in) on word beats, `XMark` strike for "not / doesn't / no", `Sparkle` for wins, `Arrow` arcs for spreading, coins flying in arcs for money flow. A 0.3 s crossfade with a slight scale between scenes, and a 4 % push-in per scene. The boil jitter and paper grain come from `PaperStage` automatically.
- `Person` rig: `kind` kid/adult, `armL/armR` angles (0 = down; a negative right-arm angle raises it outward, -150 = wave), `walk` phase for walk cycles, `handL/handR` to hold props, `mood` smile/grin/flat/o, `cap`, `stripe`, `stache`, `tie`, `shades`, `flip`.
- SFX (`public/sfx/`): `pop` on prop pops (0.35–0.5), `whoosh` on scene changes, `ding` on money/wins, `click` for calendar/UI, `impact` for stamps, `swell_hit` for lesson reveals. Use about 1 every 2 s, never stacked. Music around 0.12–0.13 under the voice.

## Known limits (tell the user honestly)
Character acting is puppet-style (no lip-sync or fluid walk cycles). Backgrounds are simple shapes. If richer scenes are wanted, generate flat illustrated backgrounds in the palette with an image tool and layer the kit characters on top.
