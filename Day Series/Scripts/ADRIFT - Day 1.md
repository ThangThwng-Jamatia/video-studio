# ADRIFT — Day 1 (9:16, ~75 s, AI voice George)
Series: you are stranded in a life raft at sea. One day per episode, every beat a real survival fact. Ends on a cliffhanger.

## Narration
You wake up in a life raft. The ship is gone. Water in every direction.
One man survived seventy-six days like this. Today is Day 1.
You open the survival kit. One and a half litres of water. Three flares. A mirror.
You're already thirsty. But the survival manuals say: drink nothing for the first twenty-four hours. Your body starts saving water on its own.
The ocean is right there. Don't drink it. Seawater is so salty, your kidneys burn more water flushing it out than it gives you. Every sip makes you thirstier.
Noon. The sun is the real killer. You close the canopy and stay in the shade.
Your instinct says paddle toward land. Don't. Rescuers search where the ship went down. So you stay.
Sunset. The temperature drops. Water steals body heat twenty-five times faster than air.
Then, near midnight, a light. A ship. Miles away.
You have three flares.
Do you fire one now?

## Fact sources
- Steven Callahan, 76 days adrift in an inflatable raft, Atlantic, 1982.
- SOLAS life-raft packs: 1.5 L of water per person, flares, signalling mirror.
- Survival guidance (US military survival manual FM 3-05.70): no water in the first 24 h except for the injured; never drink seawater; stay near the last known position.
- Water conducts heat away from the body ~25× faster than air.
