## Script
1. NARRATOR: Tonight your whole village finds out you're getting married. You're the last to know. You're the blacksmith's boy. Soot on your hands, holes in your boots. Lyra, the chief's daughter, has never said a word to you. And every girl in the village crosses the road when you pass. Then comes the harvest feast. Lyra walks past three empty benches and sits right next to you. Your face burns red. That's adrenaline, widening the blood vessels in your cheeks.
2. CHIEF: To the oath we swore the night these two were born!
3. NARRATOR: The hall roars. Everyone but you.
4. GRANDPA: Old law, boy. Promised at seven, sealed for life.
5. NARRATOR: Medieval church law really let families promise children at seven. You lean over and whisper. It's a joke. Right?
6. LYRA: I've known since I was nine.
7. NARRATOR: Your heart is pounding.
8. LYRA: You think the girls avoid you because of the soot? I started the rumor that you're cursed.
9. NARRATOR: Then the doors crash open. A rider in black. Another family swears you were promised to their daughter first. Lyra, or the oath? Comment A or B.

End vote: A = Lyra / B = the first oath (the rider's family). Day 2 resolves it.

## Facts
- Blushing: adrenaline (sympathetic response) dilates facial veins/capillaries, increasing blood flow to the cheeks (Harvard Health; Drummond, "The psychophysiology of blushing").
- Betrothal at seven: medieval canon law (Gratian's Decretum, later Gregory IX's Decretals) set 7 as the minimum age for a betrothal (sponsalia de futuro); marriage itself needed 12 (girls) / 14 (boys).
