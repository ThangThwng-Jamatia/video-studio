# LOCKDOWN — Day 1 (9:16, ~70 s, AI voice George)
Series: a zombie virus breaks out while you're at school. One day per episode. The zombie part is the scenario; every survival beat is a real fact (school lockdown protocol, fire code, emergency comms). Ends on a cliffhanger.

## Narration
Third period. Someone screams in the hallway. A kid just bit a teacher.
Ten minutes later, the teacher gets back up. And bites.
There are a thousand students in this building. If each infected bites one person every ten minutes, that number doubles. The whole school is gone in under two hours.
Today is Day 1.
Rule one: don't run into the hallway. Lock the door, lights off, and get out of sight of the window. That's a real lockdown drill.
But your classroom door opens outward. Fire code. Desks won't hold it. Wrap a belt around the closer arm at the top. Now it won't open.
Your phone shows full bars, but calls fail. Everyone's calling at once, and the network jams. Texts still get through. Text, don't call.
Noon. Something scratches at the door. Don't make a sound.
Sunset. The power dies. Then the fire alarm goes off, on backup battery.
Every one of them turns toward the sound. Toward the cafeteria, where your best friend is hiding.
Do you go get them?

## Facts & sources
- Scenario rule (fiction): a bite turns a person in ~10 min. Maths is real: doubling every 10 min, 1 → 1,024 after 10 doublings = 100 min (< 2 h) for 1,000 students.
- "Locks, Lights, Out of Sight": the Lockdown step of the Standard Response Protocol (The "I Love U Guys" Foundation), used by many US schools.
- NFPA 101 Life Safety Code: doors serving an occupant load of 50+ must swing in the direction of egress, so many classroom/assembly doors open outward and can't be barricaded by pushing furniture against them. Tying off the door-closer arm (belt/cord) is a widely taught lockdown improvisation.
- Ready.gov / FCC: during emergencies phone networks congest; texting is more likely to get through than calls ("text, don't call"). Seen at the 2013 Boston Marathon and 9/11.
- NFPA 72: fire-alarm systems need secondary (battery) power: 24 h standby + 5 min of alarm, so the alarm still sounds after a power cut.
