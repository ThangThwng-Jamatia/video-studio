# Why their zombie videos went viral and ours didn't

Videos compared: Video-43667 (farm, 61 s), Video-81674 (school, 110 s), Video-44625 (lighthouse, 110 s), Video-64742 (supermarket, 87 s) vs. our LOCKDOWN Day 1 (73 s).

## Side by side

| | THEIRS (viral) | OURS (LOCKDOWN Day 1) |
|---|---|---|
| **First line** | "If the zombie apocalypse starts while you're **[at a place]**, **don't [do the obvious thing]**." The premise, the place and a rule that goes against instinct, all in one sentence. | "Third period. Someone screams in the hallway." Mid-scene with no premise. The viewer doesn't know what the video is or why to stay. |
| **Second line** | An open loop: "One rule keeps you alive today, and you'll want to break it." / "...only if you don't make one fatal mistake." The payoff is promised for the end. | Straight into infection math (1,000 students, doubling every 10 min). It's a lecture by second 8. |
| **Story vs facts** | About 90% story. Roughly 2–3 practical ideas, each one a *decision* the hero makes ("take what nobody's fighting for", "take the stairs", "fill the bathtub"). No numbers or citations. | About 60% teaching: NFPA fire code, door closer arms, network jamming, the doubling math. Correct, but it plays like a safety video. |
| **Hero** | Contrarian: "40 people fight over the water. **Not you.**" / "30 kids bolt for the gym. **Not you.**" The viewer feels smarter than the crowd. | The hero follows the lockdown drill like everyone else. There's no "not you" moment. |
| **Middle** | A false sense of safety: they lock up and count supplies, then "**On paper**, you could survive for weeks…" | Clock beats (Noon / Sunset) and a scratch at the door. The tension stays flat. |
| **Twist** | **A human dilemma at the door**: a voice you know, a barefoot child, "someone was already inside the fence". It's a moral choice, not a tactical one. | The fire alarm pulls the zombies toward the cafeteria. A plot event, but nobody is asking *you* for anything right now. |
| **Setup → payoff** | Every ending calls back to the opening: the phone alert "Do not open it for anyone" comes back on screen at the end; Maya's private joke; the PA knows "Room 214". | No callback. The friend in the cafeteria is introduced in the last 10 s. |
| **Final question** | "**Would you open the door?** YES / NO", plus "Part 2 starts with your answer." It's binary and emotional, and you can answer in one comment. | "Do you go get them? A go / B stay locked in", plus "DAY 2 → FOLLOW". It reads like a quiz, with a weaker reason to comment. |
| **Sentence rhythm** | Staccato, with lots of 1–3 word lines: "Lock. Chain. Deadbolt. Bathtub. Sink." "You run." "Tenth floor. Eleventh. Twelfth." | Longer, explanatory sentences ("If each infected bites one person every ten minutes, that number doubles."). |
| **Visuals** | Flat 2D with big, readable **faces and expressions** (fear, suspicion). One idea per frame, uncluttered. Darkness and a single light source for tension (peephole, light under the door). | Low-poly 3D: faces are blocks and hard to read at phone size. Busy frames (tokens, desks, crowds). |
| **HUD** | Minimal: "ZOMBIE OUTBREAK / DAY 1" stamp + clock in the first 3 s, then just a small clock. Cards appear only when an item matters (WATER STORED 180 L, SURVIVAL RULE #1). | Always-on HUD: clock, RESCUE, INFECTED, PHONE %, NOISE. Too much to read on a phone. |
| **Captions** | Medium, complete phrases ("YOU TAKE THE STAIRS."), one colour accent. | Big, chopped mid-phrase ("SOMEONE / SCREAMS / IN"). |
| **Series model** | **Anthology.** Every video is a fresh *Day 1 / Part 1* in a new relatable place (farm, school, lighthouse, supermarket). Each one works as a standalone pilot, and only the hits get a Part 2. | One continuous series. Day 2 needs Day 1, so a viewer who lands on Day 2 is lost. |

### The biggest reasons, ranked
1. **The hook formula**: "If the zombie apocalypse starts while you're at X, don't Y." A relatable place gets "what would I do?" in under 2 s, and the counterintuitive *don't* makes people stay to hear why.
2. **An ending with a moral dilemma at a door, as a yes/no question.** That drives comments (everyone has an opinion), and comments drive reach. The door + knock + voice is the shared climax of all 4 videos.
3. **Story over teaching.** Their "tips" are the hero's choices, never lectures.
4. **"Not you."** The contrarian hero makes the viewer feel clever.
5. **Anthology pilots instead of one serial.** Each video is a new entry point, so the hook can be tested 10 times instead of once.
6. **Readable faces and simple frames.** Emotion carries through the character's face.

## The format (their template, in our words)
```
0–3 s   PREMISE + DON'T:  "If the zombie apocalypse starts while you're <place>, don't <obvious move>."
3–6 s   OPEN LOOP:        "One <rule/mistake> decides if you live, and you'll want to break it."
6–15 s  INCITING SIGNAL:  phone alert / radio / PA, with ONE ominous instruction (callback later)
15–25 s THE CROWD vs YOU: everyone does the obvious thing. "Not you." + why (one real reason)
25–50 s SMART MOVES:      3–4 staccato choices (take X, go Y, lock/fill/hide), each with a short card
50–60 s FALSE SAFETY:     "On paper, you could last <weeks> up here."
60–80 s THE DOOR:         sounds getting closer, then a human voice you have a reason to trust or pity
80–90 s CALLBACK + VOTE:  the opening instruction returns; "Would you <open/let in>? YES / NO. Part 2 starts with your answer."
```

# New series: WHERE IT STARTS (zombie anthology)
Every episode is a standalone **Part 1** in a new place, in second person ("you"). The ones that pop get a Part 2 that resolves the top-voted comment. HUD: "ZOMBIE OUTBREAK · DAY 1" stamp + clock only.

Episode slate (all different from their farm/school/lighthouse/supermarket):
1. **Plane at 35,000 ft**: don't go to the back. *(script below)*
2. **Subway train stuck in a tunnel**: don't get off at the next station.
3. **Hospital waiting room**: don't follow the nurses.
4. **Cruise ship**: don't go to your cabin.
5. **Ski gondola, stopped mid-air**: don't call for help.
6. **Movie theatre**: don't leave through the exit doors.
7. **Your own wedding**: don't let the guests leave.
8. **Gym, 6 AM**: don't go back for your phone.

## Ep 1: "35,000 FEET" (~85 s, Brian narrator; Nadia = Jessica, Captain = Harry)

> **NARRATOR:** If the zombie apocalypse starts while you're on a plane, don't go to the back.
> One rule keeps you alive on this flight, and every instinct will tell you to break it.
> Seat 4C. Two hours to land.
> Row 31 starts coughing.
> A flight attendant walks back with a blanket.
> She doesn't come back.
> Then the screaming starts, and two hundred people stand up.
> Everyone rushes toward the noise to help.
> Not you.
> You know the one door on this plane that will never open, and it's right in front of you.
> The cockpit. Reinforced. Locked from inside.
> So you go forward. Into the galley.
> **NADIA:** Sir, sit down!
> **NARRATOR:** Then she sees what's coming up the aisle.
> You pull the curtain. Wedge the cart. Kill the lights.
> Behind it, something hits the cart. Again. Again.
> **CAPTAIN (PA):** Crew, lock down. We're diverting. Forty minutes.
> **NARRATOR:** Forty minutes. A metal cart and a curtain.
> On paper, you can hold that long.
> Then Nadia pulls her sleeve down. Too fast.
> A scratch. Fresh. Bleeding.
> **NADIA:** Don't tell the captain. I'm the only one with the code to that door.
> **NARRATOR:** If she turns behind a locked cockpit door, nobody lands this plane.
> If you tell him, she's locked out with you, and so are they.
> Part two starts with your answer.
> Do you tell the captain? Yes or no?

Word count ≈ 205 → ~80–85 s at atempo 1.14 with the staccato pauses.

### Real facts used (kept light, story-first)
- Cockpit doors on airliners have been reinforced (bulletproof, locked from inside) since post-9/11 rules (FAA 2002, ICAO). Crew enter via a keypad code, and the pilots can deny entry.
- The galley carts are heavy (about 100 kg loaded) with brakes. A believable barricade.
- When an emergency occurs, airliners divert to the nearest suitable airport; 30–60 min is realistic over land.

### Visual plan (key shots)
1. 0 s: cabin aisle at night, red cabin lights, one silhouette standing at row 31. Stamp "ZOMBIE OUTBREAK · DAY 1" + "2:14 AM".
2. A close-up of the face in seat 4C, eyes turning back (readable expression).
3. A flight attendant walking away down the long aisle, the blanket, then empty aisle.
4. A crowd standing up and surging back; "NOT YOU." while the camera stays on you facing forward.
5. Cockpit door close-up, card "SURVIVAL RULE #1: GO TOWARD THE DOOR THAT NEVER OPENS".
6. Galley: curtain pull, cart wedge (card "CART ≈ 100 KG · BRAKES ON"), lights off.
7. Curtain bulging from impacts. A sound hit on each "Again."
8. PA speaker + "DIVERTING · 40 MIN" timer card (the callback timer runs to the end).
9. Nadia's sleeve: a slow push-in on the scratch.
10. Cockpit keypad glowing, her finger hovering.
11. End card: "PART 2 / DO YOU TELL THE CAPTAIN? / YES · NO / Part 2 starts with your answer."

### Production changes vs our current Day Series kit
- HUD down to the stamp + clock (+ one countdown when it matters). No INFECTED/NOISE/PHONE pills.
- Captions: medium complete phrases, sentence chunks, one accent colour per line.
- Characters need **readable faces** (eyes, brows, mouth). Either a bigger head on the 3D puppet with a 2D face texture, or use the flat 2D look from our paper-explainer kit (closer to the viral style).
- Darker frames and single light sources for the tension half.
- Episode ends on the yes/no card; no "DAY 2 → FOLLOW".
