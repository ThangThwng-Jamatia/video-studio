## Narration
What happens when a zombie apocalypse breaks out while you're at school? Day 2. Save your best friend, or stay hidden?
You go.
The fire alarm is still blaring, and that's your cover. By code, it has to be fifteen decibels louder than the room. It drowns out your footsteps.
But its battery only has to last five minutes. Move. Now.
Shoes off. Socks are quieter on hard floors. Phone on silent, and turn vibration off. A buzz is enough.
The alarm cuts out. Total darkness. Don't turn on your flashlight. Your eyes need about twenty minutes to adjust to the dark. One flash resets it.
Take the stairwell. Its doors are fire rated, and they close on their own.
Cafeteria. Your best friend is under a table, shaking. You grab them, and run for the walk-in freezer.
It feels like a trap. But walk-in freezers are built to open from the inside. And the thick walls swallow sound.
Midnight. Your phone is at nineteen percent.
Then, three knocks on the freezer door. And a voice. Is anyone in there?
Do you open it?

## Facts & sources
- NFPA 72 (18.4.3): public-mode audible alarm signals must be at least 15 dB above the average ambient sound level (or 5 dB above max for 60 s).
- NFPA 72 (10.6.7): secondary (battery) power must run the system 24 h in standby, then 5 min in full alarm. That's the required minimum, so the alarm can legally die after ~5 min of sounding.
- Socks/bare feet on hard floors make far less impact noise than hard-soled shoes (common stealth/tactical advice; heel strike is the main noise source).
- SRP lockdown guidance + school lockdown training: silence phones, including vibrate, because vibration is audible in a quiet room.
- Dark adaptation: rods take ~20–30 min to reach full sensitivity; bright light bleaches rhodopsin and restarts adaptation (standard vision physiology).
- IBC 2018 §1023 / NFPA 101: exit stair enclosures are fire-resistance rated (1 h, or 2 h for 4+ stories) with self-closing doors.
- Walk-in coolers/freezers are built with an interior safety release so nobody gets trapped (industry standard; required by many health/safety codes, e.g. NSF/UL 7 & manufacturer specs). Insulated panels (~10 cm foam) also damp sound.
